# Asking why

```lesson
(objective "Ask yourself why a fact is true, and explain each step of a worked example.")
(minutes 20)
(keywords "elaborative interrogation" "self-explanation")
```

Asking yourself "why?" while you study connects a new fact to what you
already know, and that helps you remember it.

**Elaborative interrogation** means asking yourself why a fact is true,
and then answering. For example, you read that camels can go many days
without drinking. You ask: why would that help a camel? You answer:
because it lives in deserts, where water is hard to find.

```sources
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: elaborative interrogation")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "You read: \"Metal spoons get hot in hot soup faster than wooden ones.\" Which is elaborative interrogation?")
  (right "Asking \"Why would metal heat up faster than wood?\" and answering it")
  (wrong "Reading the sentence again, slowly"
    "Rereading does not ask why. Elaborative interrogation means asking why it is true, then answering.")
  (wrong "Writing the sentence 5 times"
    "Writing it out repeats it without thinking about why. The point is to explain it.")
  (hint "Look for the one with the word \"why\"."))
```

---

## How well it works

The 2013 review rated elaborative interrogation as **moderately**
useful. It works best when you already know something about the topic,
so that you have something to connect the fact to.

If you have no idea why, that is fine: look it up, or ask, and you
have learned more.

```sources
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: elaborative interrogation")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "When does asking \"why?\" work best?")
  (right "When you already know a little about the topic")
  (wrong "Only when the topic is brand new to you"
    "With nothing to connect the fact to, it is hard to answer why. It works best when you know something already.")
  (wrong "Always the same, whatever you know"
    "The review found it works better when you have something you already know to connect the fact to.")
  (hint "To answer why, what do you need to connect the fact to?"))
```

## Explaining each step

**Self-explanation** means explaining to yourself how new information
fits with what you know, or why each step of a solution works. The
2013 review also rated it **moderately** useful.

When learn shows a worked example, it shows one step at a time. Before
you press for the next step, ask yourself why this step comes next.

```sources
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: self-explanation")
```

```example
(example
  (problem "Find the perimeter of a rectangle 5 inches long and 3 inches wide.")
  (step "Add the 2 long sides: 5 + 5 = 10 inches. Why? A rectangle has 2 sides of each length.")
  (step "Add the 2 short sides: 3 + 3 = 6 inches. Why? The same reason, for the other pair.")
  (step "Add the two: 10 + 6 = 16 inches. Why? The perimeter is the whole way around.")
  (answer "The perimeter is 16 inches, and you can say why each step is there. In centimeters, the steps are the same."))
```

```check
(number
  (ask "Use the same steps: a rectangle is 7 inches long and 2 inches wide. What is its perimeter, in inches?")
  (answer 18) (unit "inches") (from "7 + 7 + 2 + 2")
  (wrong-if 9 "That is one long side and one short side. A rectangle has 2 of each, all the way around.")
  (wrong-if 14 "That is only the 2 long sides (or 7 times 2, which is the area). The perimeter adds all 4 sides.")
  (hint "Add the 2 long sides, then the 2 short sides.")
  (hint "7 + 7 + 2 + 2."))
```

## Practice

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which of these is self-explanation?")
  (right "Saying why each step of a solved problem is done")
  (wrong "Copying a solved problem into your notebook"
    "Copying keeps the steps but not the reasons. Self-explanation means saying why each step is there.")
  (wrong "Looking at the answer at the back of the book"
    "That tells you the answer, but not why it is right.")
  (hint "Self-explanation is about why each step works."))
```

## This week

When you read something new this week, stop after each part and ask
yourself "why is that true?" Say or write your answer, then check it.

So: asking why, and explaining each step, connect new things to what
you know.
