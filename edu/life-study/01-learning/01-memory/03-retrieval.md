# Testing yourself

```lesson
(objective "Explain retrieval practice and what the testing effect experiment found.")
(minutes 20)
(keywords "retrieval practice" "testing effect")
```

Testing yourself is one of the best ways to learn, not only a way to
find out what you know.

To **retrieve** something is to get it back. **Retrieval practice**
means pulling what you learned out of your memory, without looking,
again and again. Answering a question, writing what you remember, and
saying an answer before you check it are all retrieval practice.

```sources
(source "Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, Make It Stick (Harvard University Press, 2014), chapter 2")
```

```check

  (right "Covering a list of words and trying to say what each one means")
  (wrong "Reading the list of words and their meanings again"

  (wrong "Copying the list of words neatly into a notebook"
    "Copying keeps the answer in view the whole time. Nothing is pulled from memory.")
  (hint "Which one makes you get the answer from your own memory?"))
```

## The experiment

In a study from 2006, college students read a short text for 7 minutes.
Then they either read it again, or wrote down all they could remember
of it. They wrote without looking and were not told what they got
right.

Some students took a final test 5 minutes later. Reading again did a
little better.

Others took the test 2 days or a week later. Then writing down what
they remembered did better.

```sources
(source "Roediger and Karpicke, Test-Enhanced Learning, Psychological Science 17(3), 2006, experiment 1")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "In the experiment, when did testing beat reading again?")
  (right "On the tests 2 days and a week later")
  (wrong "On the test 5 minutes later"
    "Five minutes later, reading again did a little better. Testing pulled ahead when the final test came days later.")
  (wrong "Never: reading again was always better"
    "Reading again won only right away. For remembering over days, testing yourself won.")
  (hint "There were 3 times for the final test. Which ones did the testing group win?"))
```

## The testing effect

Remembering something after a test on it, better than after studying
it again for the same time, is called the **testing effect**.

Notice 2 things about the experiment:

- The students got no answers back, and testing still helped.
- Reading again looked better at first. The gain from testing showed up
  later, which is when you need it.

A test is not only a way to measure what you know. Taking one is
practice that makes the memory stronger.

```sources
(source "Roediger and Karpicke, Test-Enhanced Learning, Psychological Science 17(3), 2006")
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: practice testing")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Lee says: \"Tests only measure what you know. They do not teach you anything.\" What does the testing effect say?")
  (right "Taking a test is itself practice that helps you remember")
  (wrong "Lee is right: tests only measure"
    "The students who wrote down what they remembered recalled more a week later. The test itself helped them learn.")
  (wrong "Tests help only if you get the answers back"
    "In the experiment, the students got no answers back and testing still helped. Checking your answers helps more, as the next lesson shows.")
  (hint "What happened a week later to the students who were tested?"))
```

## How learn does it

Every question in learn asks you before it shows you the answer. When
questions come back for review, learn shows each question on its own,
without the teaching words around it. You have to pull the answer from
memory.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Why does learn show a review question without the lesson's words?")
  (right "So you have to recall the answer, not read it")
  (wrong "To save space on the screen"
    "There is room for the words. Leaving them out is on purpose: recalling is what makes the memory stronger.")
  (wrong "Because review is only to check up on you"
    "Review is practice, not a check-up. Each question you recall strengthens that memory.")
  (hint "What is the name of the kind of practice in this lesson?"))
```
