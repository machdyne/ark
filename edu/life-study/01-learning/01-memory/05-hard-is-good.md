# When hard is good

```lesson
(objective "Explain why effort and mistakes, followed by feedback, help learning.")
(minutes 20)
(keywords "desirable difficulty" "feedback (on an answer)")
```

Study that takes effort often works better than study that feels easy.
Struggling a little to recall something is a sign that you are
learning, not that you are failing.

Something that makes learning feel harder and slower, but helps you
remember better later, is a **desirable difficulty**. Testing yourself
instead of rereading is one. You meet 2 more in the next unit.

Not every difficulty is desirable. If something is so hard that you
cannot do it at all, even with effort, you need to learn the steps
before it first.

```sources
(source "Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, Make It Stick (Harvard University Press, 2014), chapter 4")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Jo finds testing herself harder than rereading. What does that mean?")
  (right "The effort is part of why testing works so well")
  (wrong "Testing must be the wrong way for her"
    "Feeling harder is not a sign that it is wrong. The effort of recalling is what makes the memory stronger.")
  (wrong "She should only study in ways that feel easy"
    "Easy study, like rereading, feels good but helps less. A little struggle is often a good sign.")
  (hint "What is a desirable difficulty?"))
```

## Mistakes

When you try to recall something, sometimes you get it wrong. That is
fine, as long as you then find out what is right.

Being told whether your answer was right, and what the right answer
is, is called **feedback**. A mistake followed by feedback is part of
learning. A mistake nobody corrects can stay with you.

```sources
(source "Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, Make It Stick (Harvard University Press, 2014), chapter 4")
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: practice testing")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Ben gets 4 of 10 quiz questions wrong. What is most useful?")
  (right "Find out the right answers, and try those questions again another day")
  (wrong "Forget about it: those 4 were too hard for him"
    "Those 4 are just what he needs to work on. With feedback, the mistakes show him what to learn.")
  (wrong "Stop doing quizzes until he knows everything"
    "Quizzes are how he finds out what he does not know yet. Mistakes on them are useful, if he checks them.")
  (hint "What should follow a mistake?"))
```

---

## How learn does it

In learn, a wrong answer usually gets feedback that explains the
mistake, and you get a second try. After 2 wrong tries (or 1, when
there are only 2 answers to pick from), learn shows you the answer.

F5 gives a hint, and there can be up to 3. A question you answer after
a hint, or miss, counts as not known yet. It comes back for review the
next day, so you get to recall it on your own.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Why does learn count an answer given after a hint as not known yet?")
  (right "Getting it with help is not the same as recalling it on your own")
  (wrong "To punish you for using hints"
    "It is not a punishment. The question just comes back sooner, so you get to practice recalling it alone.")
  (wrong "Because hints always give the answer away"
    "The first hint is only a nudge. But any help means you did not recall it completely alone.")
  (hint "When does a memory get stronger: when you are helped, or when you recall it yourself?"))
```

---

## Using hints well

A hint is most useful after you have really tried.

```example
(example
  (problem "A question asks for the capital of Kenya, and you are not sure.")
  (step "Try first. Give the best answer you can, even a guess.")
  (step "If you are stuck after trying, take one hint, not all of them.")
  (step "Look at the answer and the feedback. Notice why the answer is what it is.")
  (answer "The question comes back the next day, and you try it again on your own."))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "You are stuck on a question. What is the best first move?")
  (right "Try to answer it yourself, even if you have to guess")
  (wrong "Press F5 three times right away"
    "Taking every hint at once skips the effort that helps you learn. Try first, then take one hint.")
  (wrong "Skip it, because it is too hard"
    "Trying and getting feedback is how you learn it. Skipping means it never gets practiced.")
  (hint "Effort first, help second."))
```

## Practice

```check

  (ask "Which of these is a desirable difficulty?")
  (right "Trying to recall a list before looking at it again")

    "Noise does not help memory. A desirable difficulty is effort spent on the thing you are learning.")
  (wrong "A book far too hard for you to understand at all"

  (hint "A desirable difficulty is effort that you can manage, spent on the thing itself."))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "What is feedback on an answer?")
  (right "Being told whether you were right, and what the right answer is")
  (wrong "Your score at the end of a test"
    "A score tells you how many you got right, but not which ones or why. Feedback tells you what is right.")
  (wrong "A hint before you answer"
    "A hint comes before your answer. Feedback comes after it.")
  (hint "Feedback comes after you answer."))
```

## This week

The next time a question feels hard, try it before you look anything
up. Then check, and write down the right answer next to your try.

So: effort that you can manage, followed by feedback on your mistakes,
makes learning last.
