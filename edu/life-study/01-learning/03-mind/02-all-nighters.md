# Staying up to cram

```lesson
(objective "Explain why staying up late to cram costs more than it gains.")
(minutes 15)
```

Staying up late to cram loses twice: you get massed practice instead of
spaced practice, and you lose the sleep that helps you keep it.

Here is what each part costs:

- **Cramming** is massed practice. It can feel like it works the next
  morning, but it fades fast.
- **Losing sleep** takes away time for consolidation. Regular short
  sleep also goes with problems in attention and learning.

```sources
(source "Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, Make It Stick (Harvard University Press, 2014), chapter 3")
(source "Paruthi and others, Recommended Amount of Sleep for Pediatric Populations, Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine 12(6), 2016")
(source "Diekelmann and Born, The Memory Function of Sleep, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 11(2), 2010")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Why is staying up late to cram a poor plan?")
  (right "It is massed practice, and it takes away sleep that helps memory")
  (wrong "It is a fine plan if you drink something sweet to stay awake"
    "Staying awake is not the problem it solves. The practice is still massed, and the sleep is still lost.")
  (wrong "Studying at night is always worse than in the day"
    "The time of day is not the main problem. Cramming it all in one go, and losing sleep, are.")
  (hint "The lesson named 2 costs."))
```

---

## The fix: start earlier

The way out is not to study less. It is to start sooner, so that the
night before the test is light.

```example
(example
  (problem "Rosa has a history test on Friday. She has a list of 15 dates and events.")
  (step "Monday: she learns the list and tests herself until each one is right once.")
  (step "Wednesday: she tests herself on the whole list, mixed up, and practices the ones she missed.")
  (step "Thursday evening: one short self-test, then bed at her usual time.")
  (answer "Spaced practice, a short check the night before, and a full night's sleep."))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "What is the best thing to do the night before a test?")
  (right "A short self-test, then go to bed at your usual time")
  (wrong "Read all your notes from the start, however late it gets"
    "Rereading late into the night is massed practice and costs sleep. A short self-test shows what to look at, then sleep.")
  (wrong "Nothing at all, not even a quick check"
    "A short self-test is useful: it shows you what still needs a look. Just keep it short and sleep on time.")
  (hint "Think about both practice and sleep."))
```

---

## When you are already behind

Sometimes you start late anyway. Then, test yourself instead of
rereading, so your time goes where it helps most. Work on what you got
wrong, and still keep most of your night's sleep.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Leo has 1 evening left before a test, and has not studied. What is his best plan?")
  (right "Test himself, work on what he got wrong, and still sleep most of the night")
  (wrong "Reread the whole chapter twice, however long it takes"
    "Rereading is slow and works poorly. Testing himself shows him what to work on, and leaves time for sleep.")
  (wrong "Give up, because it is too late to do anything"
    "One evening of testing himself is much better than nothing. He can still learn a good amount.")
  (hint "Use the best method for the time he has, and protect his sleep."))
```

## Practice

```check
(choice
  (ask "Sara stayed up until 2:00 cramming and did well on the test the next day. What is likely to happen later?")
  (right "Much of what she crammed will fade quickly")
  (wrong "She will remember it all, because she did well"
    "Doing well the next morning does not mean it will last. Massed practice fades fast.")
  (wrong "Nothing: cramming and spacing work the same in the end"
    "Spacing helps people remember more over time. Cramming fades faster.")
  (hint "What happens to massed practice over the following weeks?"))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which plan for a test in a week protects sleep best?")
  (right "Three 20-minute sessions on different days")
  (wrong "One 60-minute session the night before, after the usual bedtime"
    "That is massed practice and costs an hour of sleep. Spread it out instead.")
  (wrong "No study, and an extra hour of sleep every night"
    "Sleep helps keep what you studied, but there must be something to keep.")
  (hint "Look for spacing and normal bedtimes."))
```

---

## This week

Look at your next test or project. Put 3 short study sessions on the
calendar before the day, and write your usual bedtime next to the last
one.

So: start early, space it out, and keep your sleep, instead of
cramming late.
