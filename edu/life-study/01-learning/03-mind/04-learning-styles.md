# Learning styles

```lesson
(objective "Explain what research found about learning styles.")
(minutes 15)
(keywords "learning styles")
```

Many people believe that each person learns best in one way, but
research has not found good evidence for it.

**Learning styles** is the idea that each person has one best way to
learn, such as by seeing, by hearing, or by doing. The idea says you
learn more when you are taught in your own style.

Many teachers and students believe this. It is worth knowing what the
research found.

```sources
(source "Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer and Bjork, Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 9(3), 2008")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "What does the learning styles idea say?")
  (right "Each person learns more when taught in their own best way, such as by seeing or hearing")
  (wrong "Everyone learns best by testing themselves"
    "That is the testing effect, which has strong evidence. Learning styles is a different idea, about matching teaching to each person.")
  (wrong "Some subjects are easier than others"
    "That may be true, but it is not the learning styles idea. That idea is about matching teaching to each person's style.")
  (hint "The idea is about matching teaching to the person."))
```

---

## What the research found

In 2008, 4 researchers looked for the evidence. To show the idea is
true, a study must teach people of different styles in different ways.
It must find that "seeing" learners learn more with pictures, and
"hearing" learners learn more by listening.

Very few studies tested it that way. Several of the ones that did found
results that went against the idea. The researchers concluded there was no good evidence for
using learning styles in schools.

```sources
(source "Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer and Bjork, Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 9(3), 2008")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "What did the 2008 review conclude about learning styles?")
  (right "There was no good evidence for using them in schools")
  (wrong "They are proven, because so many people believe in them"
    "Lots of people believing an idea is not evidence for it. The review found very few studies of the right kind, and several went against the idea.")
  (wrong "Everyone has the same style, so it does not matter"
    "The review did not say people are all the same. It said that teaching to a person's style has not been shown to help.")
  (hint "Think about the kind of study needed, and what such studies found."))
```

---

## What is true

People do have preferences: you may enjoy pictures more than
reading, or the other way around. That is real. What has not been shown
is that teaching to your preference makes you learn more.

What helps is matching the way of showing something to the thing
itself. A map is best seen. A song is best heard. That is true for
everyone, whatever their style.

```sources
(source "Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer and Bjork, Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 9(3), 2008")
(source "Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, Make It Stick (Harvard University Press, 2014), chapter 6")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which is the best way to learn where the countries of Africa are?")
  (right "A map, for everyone, whatever they prefer")
  (wrong "A map for seeing learners, and a spoken list for hearing learners"
    "Where countries are is something you see. A map suits the thing being learned, for everybody.")
  (wrong "Whatever way each person enjoys most"
    "Enjoying a way of learning is nice, but it does not make it work better. Match the way to what you learn.")
  (hint "Match the way of showing it to the thing itself."))
```

---

## What it means for you

Do not tell yourself "I am a seeing learner, so I cannot learn by
listening." You can learn in many ways.

Whatever you prefer, the methods with strong evidence work: testing
yourself, spacing your practice, and getting enough sleep.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Amir says he is a \"doing\" learner, so flashcards will not work for him. What would you tell him?")
  (right "Testing yourself helps all kinds of learners; there is no good evidence it depends on your style")
  (wrong "He is right: he should only learn by doing"
    "There is no good evidence that matching your style helps. Testing yourself has strong evidence for all kinds of learners.")
  (wrong "He should find out his real style first"
    "Even if he knew a style, research has not shown that teaching to it helps. He can just use methods that work.")
  (hint "Which methods have strong evidence for everyone?"))
```

## This week

Notice a time this week when you think "I am not good at learning this
way." Try a method with strong evidence instead: close the book and
test yourself.

So: you may prefer some ways of learning, but there is no good
evidence that matching them helps, while testing and spacing help all
kinds of learners.
