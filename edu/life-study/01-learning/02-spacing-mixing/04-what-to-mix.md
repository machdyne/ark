# What to mix

```lesson
(objective "Choose what to mix: kinds of problems you must learn to tell apart.")
(minutes 15)
```

Mixing seems to help most with kinds of problems that look alike but
need different methods: the ones you have to learn to tell apart.

Most of the evidence for interleaving comes from math problems. Some
comes from learning to tell kinds of things apart, like the styles of
different painters.

```sources
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: interleaved practice")
(source "Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, Make It Stick (Harvard University Press, 2014), chapter 3")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which set of problems is most worth mixing?")
  (right "Area and perimeter problems, which both use the sides of a shape")
  (wrong "Spelling words and the dates of wars"
    "These are so different that you never mix them up. Mixing helps most with things you could confuse.")
  (wrong "20 copies of the same kind of problem"
    "That is all one kind, so there is nothing to tell apart. Mixing needs at least 2 kinds.")
  (hint "Which 2 kinds could you easily mix up on a test?"))
```

## Learn each kind first

Mixing is for practice, after you know how to do each kind. In the
2007 experiment, students were shown how to solve each kind of shape
before they practiced it, blocked or mixed.

When something is brand new, a few problems of one kind can help you
see how it works. Then start mixing it with the others.

```sources
(source "Rohrer and Taylor, The Shuffling of Mathematics Problems Improves Learning, Instructional Science 35, 2007")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Nina has just met long division for the first time today. What should she do?")
  (right "Try a few long division problems first, then mix them with other kinds later")
  (wrong "Mix long division with 5 other kinds right away"
    "She does not know how to do it yet, so mixing is likely to confuse her. Learn it first, then mix.")
  (wrong "Never mix long division with anything"
    "Once she can do it, mixing it with other kinds will help her choose the right method on a test.")
  (hint "Mixing is for practice after you know how to do each kind."))
```

## Mixing also spaces

When you mix, the problems of one kind are spread out in the session.
You meet an area problem, then other kinds, then area again. So mixing
also adds some spacing.

```example
(example
  (problem "You have learned 3 kinds of word problems this month: sharing, comparing and rates. Plan a review.")
  (step "Pick 3 problems of each kind from your old homework.")
  (step "Shuffle them, so the kinds are mixed.")
  (step "For each one, first write which kind it is, then solve it.")
  (step "Check each answer. Note which kinds you mixed up.")
  (answer "9 mixed problems, each starting with a choice, and a list of what to practice next."))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "In a mixed set, why is there spacing too?")
  (right "Problems of one kind are spread out, with other kinds between them")
  (wrong "Because mixed sets always take more days"
    "A mixed set can be done in one sitting. The spacing is inside the set: other problems come between those of one kind.")
  (wrong "There is no spacing in a mixed set"
    "In a mixed set, problems of one kind have others between them. That is a small gap each time.")
  (hint "Look at where the area problems are in a mixed list."))
```

## How sure is the evidence?

The 2013 review rated interleaving as **moderately** useful, not as high
as testing yourself and spacing. There are fewer studies, most of them
are about math, and not all of them found a benefit.

So: use it, especially for problems you mix up, and keep testing
yourself and spacing as your main tools.

```sources
(source "Dunlosky and others, Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 2013: interleaved practice")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "How did the 2013 review rate interleaving?")
  (right "Moderately useful, with less research than testing and spacing")
  (wrong "The most useful way of all to study"
    "The review rated testing yourself and spacing highest. Interleaving was rated moderate.")
  (wrong "Not useful at all"
    "The review found good results for interleaving, but from fewer studies and fewer subjects, so it rated it moderate.")
  (hint "It was rated between high and low."))
```
