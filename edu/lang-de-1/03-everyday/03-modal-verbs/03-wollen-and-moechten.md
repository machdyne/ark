# wollen and möchten

```lesson
(objective "Say what you want with wollen and möchten plus an infinitive, and not mistake will for \"will\".")
(minutes 25)
```

In this lesson you say what you want to do: **Ich will ins Kino gehen.** (I want to go to the movies.)

**wollen** means "to want". It is a modal verb: the infinitive goes to the end.

| | wollen |
|---|---|
| ich | will |
| du | willst |
| er, sie, es | will |
| wir | wollen |
| ihr | wollt |
| sie, Sie | wollen |

```cards
(card "wollen" "to want" (typed) (note "ich will, du willst, er will: want, not the English will"))
```

```check
(text
  (ask "Du ___ tanzen. Type the form of wollen.")
  (accept "willst")
  (wrong-if "will" "Will is for ich and er. For du, add -st: willst.")
  (wrong-if "wollst" "The singular changes the vowel: du willst.")
  (hint "ich will, du ..."))
```

---

## will is not "will"

**Ich will** looks like English "I will", but it means **I want**. It says nothing about the future.

> Ich will Deutsch lernen. (I want to learn German.)

```check
(choice
  (ask "Er will Tennis spielen. What does it mean?")
  (right "He wants to play tennis.")
  (wrong "He will play tennis."
    "German will is not the English will. It comes from wollen: he wants to.")
  (wrong "He can play tennis."
    "Can is kann, from können. Will comes from wollen, to want.")
  (hint "will comes from wollen."))
```

---

## möchten with an infinitive

You know **möchten** with a thing: Ich möchte ein Eis. It is a modal verb, so it also takes an infinitive at the end:

> Ich möchte ins Kino gehen. (I would like to go to the movies.)

**will** says it straight; **möchte** is softer and more polite. To ask a stranger for something, say möchte: **Ich will bezahlen** can sound blunt in a café; **Ich möchte bezahlen** is polite.

```cards
(card "ins Kino gehen" "to go to the movies" (typed))
(card "der Film, die Filme" "the movie" (typed) (note "Also: the film."))
(card "das Konzert, die Konzerte" "the concert" (typed) (note "ins Konzert gehen: to go to a concert"))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "In a café, which is the polite way to say you want to pay?")
  (right "Ich möchte bezahlen.")
  (wrong "Ich will bezahlen."
    "Will is right in meaning, but it can sound blunt to a waiter. Möchte is the polite choice.")
  (wrong "Ich möchte bezahle."
    "The second verb stays in the infinitive: bezahlen.")
  (hint "the softer modal verb."))
```

## A worked example

```example
(example
  (problem "Say in German: We want to see a movie.")
  (step "wir takes wollen: wir wollen.")
  (step "der Film as an object: einen Film.")
  (step "The infinitive sehen goes to the end.")
  (answer "Wir wollen einen Film sehen."))
```

## Plans for today

> Nina: Was willst du heute machen?

> Ben: Ich will ins Kino gehen. Ich will einen Film sehen.

> Nina: Ich möchte lieber ins Konzert gehen. (rather)

> Ben: Ein Konzert? Das ist teuer!

> Nina: Gut, dann gehen wir ins Kino.

```check
(choice
  (ask "What do Nina and Ben do in the end?")
  (right "They go to the movies.")
  (wrong "They go to a concert."
    "Nina would like a concert, but Ben finds it expensive. In the end: dann gehen wir ins Kino.")
  (wrong "They stay at home."
    "Nina's last line: dann gehen wir ins Kino.")
  (hint "Read the last line."))
```

## Practice

```check
(text
  (ask "Wir ___ Deutsch lernen. Type the form of wollen.")
  (accept "wollen")
  (wrong-if "will" "Will is for ich and er. For wir it is wollen.")
  (wrong-if "wollt" "Wollt is for ihr. For wir it is wollen.")
  (hint "wir: the infinitive form."))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which sentence is right?")
  (right "Willst du einen Film sehen?")
  (wrong "Willst du sehen einen Film?"
    "The infinitive goes to the end: Willst du einen Film sehen?")
  (wrong "Willst du ein Film sehen?"
    "Der Film is masculine and the object, so ein becomes einen.")
  (hint "Infinitive last; der Film as an object."))
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Ich will heute kochen. What does it mean?")
  (right "I want to cook today.")
  (wrong "I will cook today."
    "Ich will means I want. German will never means the English will.")
  (hint "wollen = to want."))
```

```check
(text
  (ask "Er ___ einen Kaffee trinken. (would like) Type the form.")
  (accept "möchte")
  (wrong-if "möchtet" "Möchtet is for ihr. For er it is möchte.")
  (wrong-if "möchtest" "Möchtest is for du. For er it is möchte.")
  (hint "Like ich: möchte."))
```

Now you can say what you want with wollen and möchte plus an infinitive, and you know that will means want.
