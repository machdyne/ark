# Primary and secondary sources

```lesson
(objective "Tell a primary from a secondary source, and say what each is good for.")
(minutes 15)
(keywords "primary source" "secondary source")
```

You can tell a first-hand source from one that reports on it.

A **primary source** comes from the time or the people it is about: a
diary, a photo taken at the event, a letter, the data from an
experiment, a law as written.

A **secondary source** is made later, from primary sources: a history
book, a news article about a study, an encyclopedia entry, a
documentary.

```sources
(source "Library of Congress, pages for teachers on using primary sources")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which is a primary source about a soccer game?")
  (right "A video recording of the game itself")
  (wrong "A book about the history of the club"
    "A history book is written later, from other sources. That makes it secondary.")
  (wrong "An encyclopedia entry on the sport"
    "An encyclopedia sums up other sources. It is secondary.")
  (hint "Which one comes from the event itself?"))
```

---

## What each is good for

Primary sources are closest to what happened, but they can be one-sided:
one diary shows one person's view. Secondary sources can compare many
primary sources and explain them, but they can also make mistakes in
passing things on.

When a news story says "a study found ...", the story is secondary.
If the claim matters, look for the study itself, or at least check
what it really says.

```check
(choice
  (ask "A news article says a study found that a made-up drink \"doubles energy\". What is the best next step?")
  (right "Find what the study itself says")
  (wrong "Trust the article: it reports a study"
    "The article is secondary. Claims are often simplified or changed in passing on: check the primary source.")
  (wrong "Ignore it: news is always wrong"
    "News is not always wrong. But going to the study itself lets you see exactly what was found.")
  (hint "Which is the primary source here?"))
```

---

## Worked example

```example
(example
  (problem "You want to know what a town's council decided about a new park. Name a primary and a secondary source.")
  (step "Primary: the council's own record of the meeting, written at the time.")
  (step "Secondary: a newspaper article about the decision.")
  (answer "The record is primary; the article is secondary and may leave things out."))
```

## Practice

```check
(choice
  (ask "A scientist's lab notebook from her experiments is what kind of source?")
  (right "Primary")
  (wrong "Secondary"
    "The notebook was written at the time, by the person doing the work. That is a primary source.")
  (hint "Was it made at the time, by the person involved?"))
```

---

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which is a secondary source?")
  (right "A school textbook chapter on the history of flight")
  (wrong "A letter a pilot wrote home after her first flight"
    "The pilot wrote it at the time about her own flight: a primary source.")
  (wrong "A photo taken at an early airshow"
    "A photo from the event itself is primary.")
  (hint "Which was made later, from other sources?"))
```

So: primary sources come from the time or the people involved;
secondary sources report and explain them later.
