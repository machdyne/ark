# Lateral reading

```lesson
(objective "Check a website by reading what others say about it.")
(minutes 15)
(keywords "lateral reading")
```

You can check a website quickly by leaving it.

A website can look professional and still mislead: anyone can make a
neat page with a smart name and an "About us" that praises itself.
Reading only the site itself, top to bottom, tells you what it wants
you to think.

**Lateral reading** means leaving the site early and opening new tabs
to find out what other sources say about it: who runs it, who pays for
it, and whether others trust it. "Lateral" means sideways.

```check
(choice
  (ask "You find a made-up site, \"The Healthy Family Institute\". What is lateral reading?")
  (right "Searching for the site's name elsewhere to see who runs it")
  (wrong "Reading its \"About us\" page carefully"
    "\"About us\" is written by the site itself. Lateral reading means checking what others say.")
  (wrong "Reading every page of the site"
    "Reading more of the site tells you only what it says about itself.")
  (hint "Lateral means sideways: out of the site."))
```

---

## Why it works

Researchers at Stanford watched professional fact-checkers, historians
and students judge websites. The fact-checkers did best. They read
less of each site and left it sooner, to see what others said about
it.

```sources
(source "Wineburg, S. and McGrew, S. (2019), \"Lateral Reading and the Nature of Expertise: Reading Less and Learning More When Evaluating Digital Information\", Teachers College Record 121(11)")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "What did the fact-checkers do differently?")
  (right "They left a site quickly to see what other sources said about it")
  (wrong "They read every page of the site very carefully"
    "Careful reading of the site alone is what the others did. The fact-checkers read less of it and checked elsewhere.")
  (wrong "They judged by how professional the site looked"
    "A neat design is easy to make. The fact-checkers looked past it.")
  (hint "Which way did they read: down, or sideways?"))
```

---

## Worked example

```example
(example
  (problem "A made-up site, \"Clean Water Facts\", says a water filter removes everything harmful. How do you read laterally?")
  (step "Open a new tab and search for \"Clean Water Facts\".")
  (step "You find that the site is run by the company that sells the filter.")
  (answer "The site has a reason to praise the filter: look for independent tests before you believe it."))
```

## Practice

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which is the best first step to check a site you have never heard of?")
  (right "Search its name and read what reliable sources say about it")
  (wrong "Check whether it looks modern"
    "Anyone can make a modern-looking site. Looks tell you little about trust.")
  (wrong "See if it has a lot of pages"
    "A big site is not more reliable. Find out who is behind it.")
  (hint "Leave the site."))
```

---

```check
(choice
  (ask "A site's \"About us\" says it is \"independent and unbiased\". What should you do?")
  (right "Check what others say about who runs and pays for it")
  (wrong "Trust it: it says it is independent"
    "Any site can say that about itself. That is circular: trusting the site because the site says so.")
  (hint "Who is telling you it is independent?"))
```

So: to judge a site, leave it: read laterally to see who is behind it.
