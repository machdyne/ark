# Other people's work

```lesson
(objective "Use pictures and other work made by others only with permission or when the maker allows it, and say who made it.")
(standards "1B-IC-21")
(minutes 20)
(keywords "permission (to use)" "public domain" "Creative Commons")
```

Pictures, music and stories online were made by someone, so you check
before you use them.

Every picture, song, story or game was made by a person. It is their
work. Copying it and saying you made it is not fair. Often it is also
against the rules, unless the maker says yes.

To use someone's work, you can ask for **permission**: you ask the
maker, and they say yes.

```sources
(source "U.S. Copyright Office, Circular 1: Copyright Basics")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Noah finds a cool drawing of a dragon online. He wants to put it in his own game. What should he do first?")
  (right "Find out if the maker lets people use it, or ask for permission")
  (wrong "Use it: everything online is free to use"
    "Being online does not make a picture free to use. Someone made the dragon, and it is their work.")
  (wrong "Use it and say he drew it"
    "That is not fair to the person who drew it. He needs permission, and he should say who made it.")
  (hint "Who made the dragon?"))
```

## Work anyone may use

Some work is free for everyone to use. Work in the **public domain**
belongs to everyone: anyone may use it, for anything, without asking.
Many very old stories, songs and pictures are in the public domain.

Some makers give their work to the public domain on purpose. The
makers of this course did: it is marked CC0, so anyone may copy it and
share it.

```sources
(source "Creative Commons: CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication")
(source "U.S. Copyright Office, Circular 1: Copyright Basics (public domain)")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "A picture is in the public domain. What do you need to do before you use it?")
  (right "Nothing: anyone may use it")
  (wrong "Ask the maker for permission"
    "Public domain means it belongs to everyone. You do not need to ask, though it is still kind to say who made it.")
  (wrong "Pay for it"
    "Public domain work is free for everyone to use.")
  (hint "Who does public domain work belong to?"))
```

---

## Using it with rules

Some makers mark their work with **Creative Commons**. This means you
may use it if you follow the rules written with it. A rule that almost
every Creative Commons mark has: say who made the work.

```sources
(source "Creative Commons: About CC Licenses")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "A song is marked Creative Commons. Its rule says: say who made it. Lily uses it in her video. What must she do?")
  (right "Write the maker's name in her video")
  (wrong "Nothing: Creative Commons means no rules"
    "Creative Commons work comes with rules. This one says to give the maker's name.")
  (wrong "She may not use it at all"
    "She may use it. Creative Commons lets her, as long as she follows its rule.")
  (hint "What does the rule say?"))
```

## Worked example

```example
(example
  (problem "Kai wants a picture of a cat for his school poster. How can he use one fairly?")
  (step "He can draw his own cat.")
  (step "Or he can find a picture in the public domain, or with a Creative Commons mark, and follow its rules.")
  (step "Or he can ask the person who made a picture for permission.")
  (answer "Whichever picture he uses, he writes who made it on the poster."))
```

```example
(example
  (problem "Ella takes a photo of her friend Ben. She wants to share it online.")
  (step "Ben is in the photo, so it is about him too.")
  (step (choice (ask "What should Ella do first?")
    (right "Ask Ben if he is happy for her to share it")
    (wrong "Share it: she took the photo" "Ella took the photo, but Ben is in it. Ask him first, and a trusted adult too.")))
  (answer "Ella asks Ben first. Asking first is fair to the people in a photo, too."))
```

## Practice

```check
(choice
  (several)
  (ask "Which of these are fair ways to use other people's work? Choose every one.")
  (right "Using a public domain picture")
  (right "Asking the maker and getting a yes")
  (right "Following the rules of a Creative Commons mark")
  (wrong "Copying a story and saying you wrote it"
    "That is not fair to the writer. Say who wrote it, and use it only if the writer allows it.")
  (hint "Three of these are fair. One takes the credit for someone else's work."))
```

So: ask for permission, or use public domain or Creative Commons work
by its rules, and always say who made it.
