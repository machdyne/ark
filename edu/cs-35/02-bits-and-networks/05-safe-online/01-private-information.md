# Private information

```lesson
(objective "Tell private information from what is safe to share, and say what to do when someone online asks for it.")
(standards "1B-NI-05")
(minutes 20)
(keywords "private information" "trusted adult" "online")
```

Some facts about you are private, and you keep them to yourself when
you are online.

You are **online** when your computer, tablet or phone is joined to
the Internet: for a game, a video or a message.

**Private information** is facts about you that a stranger could use to
find you, or to pretend to be you. Here are some:

- your full name together with where you live;
- your home address or your phone number;
- the name of your school;
- your birthday: the day, month and year;
- where you are right now.

```sources
(source "Federal Trade Commission (US), Consumer Advice: advice on protecting kids online")
(source "NetSmartz, National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (US): online safety for kids")
```

```check
(choice
  (several)
  (ask "Which of these are private information? Choose every one.")
  (right "Your home address")
  (right "The name of your school")
  (right "Your phone number")
  (wrong "Your favorite color"
    "Your favorite color cannot help anyone find you or pretend to be you. It is fine to share.")
  (hint "Could a stranger use it to find you?"))
```

---

## What is fine to share

Not everything is private. Your favorite color, a book you liked or a
drawing of a dragon cannot help anyone find you.

Pictures can show private things by mistake. A photo might show your
house number, a street sign or your school's name on your shirt.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Leo wants to share a photo of his new bike. Behind the bike you can read his house number and street name. What should he do?")
  (right "Take a new photo without the house number and street")
  (wrong "Share it: it is only a bike"
    "The bike is fine, but the house number and street name say where Leo lives. That is private information.")
  (wrong "Share it, but only with people who say they are his age"
    "Anyone can say they are his age. The photo still shows where he lives.")
  (hint "What else can people see in the photo?"))
```

---

## A trusted adult

A **trusted adult** is a grown-up you know well, who looks after you:
a parent, a grandparent, a teacher. If you are not sure whether something is
private, ask a trusted adult first.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which person is a trusted adult?")
  (right "Your teacher at school")
  (wrong "A grown-up you met in an online game"
    "You have never met this person, and you cannot be sure who they are. A trusted adult is someone you know well in real life.")
  (wrong "A friend from your class"
    "A friend can help, but a trusted adult is a grown-up who looks after you, like a parent, a grandparent or a teacher.")
  (hint "A grown-up you know well, who looks after you."))
```

---

## When someone asks

Someone you know only online is a stranger. That is true even when they
are friendly, or say they are your age. You cannot be sure who they are.

```warn
(warn "If someone online asks"
  "If someone you know only online asks for private information, do not answer. Do not agree to meet them. Tell a trusted adult. You are not in trouble for telling.")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "A friendly player in a game asks Mia which school she goes to. What should Mia do?")
  (right "Not answer, and tell a trusted adult")
  (wrong "Answer, because the player is friendly"
    "Being friendly does not tell Mia who the player really is. Her school is private information.")
  (wrong "Make up a school name and keep chatting"
    "It is better not to answer at all, and to tell a trusted adult. They can help if the player keeps asking.")
  (hint "Is a school name private? Who should Mia tell?"))
```

---

## Practice

```check
(choice
  (ask "A website for a contest asks for your full name, home address and phone number. What do you do first?")
  (right "Ask a trusted adult before typing anything")
  (wrong "Type them in, because you want to win"
    "Your address and phone number are private. A trusted adult can check if the website is safe, and decide with you.")
  (wrong "Type just your address"
    "Your address alone is private information too. Ask a trusted adult first.")
  (hint "Name, address and phone: are they private?"))
```

So: keep private information to yourself online, and when you are
unsure or someone asks, tell a trusted adult.
